## Temperature: A Knob for Randomness

<Convo
  lines={[
    { who: 'cherry', text: 'The same prompt gives me different answers every time. Can I turn that down?' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'Turn down the temperature. It is a hyperparameter that controls the degree of randomness of a model’s output. Higher temperatures give more random output and lower temperatures give less random output. The best value depends on the application.' },
    { who: 'sundu', text: 'Where does the number go?' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'In the softmax. You divide the model’s raw scores, the logits, by the temperature t before turning them into probabilities.' },
  ]}
/>

<Gif
  name="temperature"
  alt="Five candidate next words for the phrase I would like a cup of are shown as bars. At a low temperature the top word has most of the probability. As the temperature rises the bars flatten until all five words have similar odds. The probabilities are a toy example."
  caption="Fig. 129: Toy odds. Low temperature sharpens, high temperature flattens."
/>

<Convo
  lines={[
    { who: 'sundu', text: 'Who studied what that does to text?' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'Holtzman, Buys, Du, Forbes and Choi, in The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration. They note that even though likelihood works as a training objective, using it as a decoding objective leads to text that is bland and strangely repetitive. They find that decoding strategies alone can dramatically affect the quality of machine text, even from exactly the same model.' },
    { who: 'cherry', text: 'And temperature?' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'They write the softmax with the logits divided by the temperature t. Setting t between 0 and 1 skews the distribution towards high-probability events, which implicitly lowers the mass in the tail. They also note earlier analysis showing that lowering the temperature improves generation quality, but at the cost of decreasing diversity.' },
    { who: 'sundu', text: 'So there is a tradeoff.' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'Yes, and their own fix was a different one, nucleus sampling: sample from the dynamic nucleus of the probability distribution, truncating the unreliable tail while keeping diversity.' },
  ]}
/>

<CaseStudy
  title="The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration (Holtzman et al., 2019)"
  stats={[
    { value: 't below 1', label: 'Skews the distribution toward high-probability words' },
    { value: 'Quality up, diversity down', label: 'The tradeoff the paper notes for lowering temperature' },
  ]}
  winner="The abstract: “decoding strategies alone can dramatically effect the quality of machine text, even when generated from exactly the same neural language model.”"
  takeaway="Temperature is a decoding choice, not a model change. It trades safety for variety, and you choose the point on that dial."
  source={{
    text: 'Holtzman, Buys, Du, Forbes and Choi, “The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration”, 2019. Free on arXiv.',
    href: 'https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09751',
  }}
/>

**Real definition:** a hyperparameter that controls the degree of randomness of a model’s output. Higher temperatures result in more random output, while lower temperatures result in less random output. [Google’s glossary: temperature ↗](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/glossary#temperature)

## Tree-of-Thought Prompting: Think, Backtrack, Think Again

<Convo
  lines={[
    { who: 'cherry', text: 'Chain of thought from Part C helps, but when the first step is wrong the whole answer is wrong.' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'That is what tree-of-thought prompting addresses. Google describes it as a prompting strategy that encourages an LLM to pursue and refine the most promising intermediate solutions and abandon the rest. You divide a problem into branches, have the model evaluate each branch after each step, keep the promising ones, and backtrack if a promising branch fails.' },
    { who: 'sundu', text: 'Like a person with a pencil crossing things out.' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'Exactly like that.' },
  ]}
/>

<Gif
  name="tree-of-thought"

Two other branches are marked red because they cannot reach 24 and are pruned. A caption cites the paper's results of 4 percent for chain of thought and 74 percent for tree of thoughts."
  caption="Fig. 132: A real Game of 24 instance. Prune the dead ends, keep the live branch."
/>

<Convo
  lines={[
    { who: 'sundu', text: 'Who tested it?' },
    { who: 'sriku', text: 'Yao, Yu, Zhao, Shafran, Griffiths, Cao and Narasimhan. The model considers multiple reasoning paths, self-evaluates its choices, and can look ahead or backtrack.' },
    { who: 'cherry', text: 'On what tasks?' },
